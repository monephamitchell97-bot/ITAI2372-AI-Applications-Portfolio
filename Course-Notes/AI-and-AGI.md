# Artificial Intelligence and Artificial General Intelligence

## Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. Examples include recognizing images, analyzing data, answering questions, and making predictions.

Most current AI systems are designed for specific tasks.

## Artificial General Intelligence

Artificial General Intelligence, or AGI, refers to a theoretical form of AI that could understand, learn, and perform many different intellectual tasks at a human-like level.

## Main Difference

Traditional AI is usually designed for a narrow purpose, while AGI would be able to adapt its knowledge and reasoning across different situations.

## Possible AGI Benefits

- Solving complex problems
- Supporting advanced research
- Improving decision-making
- Connecting information from multiple systems
- Adapting to unfamiliar situations

## Possible Risks

- Cybersecurity threats
- Loss of human control
- Privacy concerns
- Unreliable decisions
- Unclear accountability
- Overdependence on technology

## Key Learning

AGI could create major opportunities, but it would require strong testing, regulation, security, ethical standards, and human oversight.
